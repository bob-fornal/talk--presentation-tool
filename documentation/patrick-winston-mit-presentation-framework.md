# Patrick Winston's MIT Presentation Framework

* [VIDEO](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pifBV1Fthu4)

Patrick Winston’s MIT presentation framework, detailed in his famous "How to Speak" lecture, focuses on structured, memorable communication rather than charisma. Key elements include starting with an "empowerment promise" (telling the audience what they will learn), using the "5S" rule to make ideas stick (Symbol, Slogan, Surprise, Salient, Story), and ending with a contribution rather than a summary.

Core Components of the Winston Framework

* The Opening (Empowerment Promise): Never start with a joke. Within the first 60 seconds, promise the audience what they will know or be able to do by the end.
* The 5S Rule (Making Ideas Stick):

    * Symbol: A visual or metaphor representing the core idea.
    * Slogan: A short, memorable phrase capturing the idea.
    * Surprise: A surprising fact or angle to grab attention.
    * Salient Idea: A sharp, clear focal point.
    * Story: A narrative that illustrates the idea.
* The "Near Miss" Technique: Rather than just showing the right answer, demonstrate what almost works to help the audience understand the concept deeply.
* Structure: Organize talks around a clear vision, proof of work, and contributions.
* Ending (Contribution): End by explaining what the audience gained, rather than summarizing what was said.
* Presentation Style: Use whiteboards for a steady, engaging pace that matches human absorption rates. Use physical props to illustrate concepts.
* Slide Crimes: Avoid slides with excessive text or that act as teleprompters.
